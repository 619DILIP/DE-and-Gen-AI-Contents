### Completion Time
45 minutes

### Lecture Focus
- Outline the differences between severl different SDLC workflows, highlighting the differences between iterative and linear styles.
- Introduce some linear workflows, like V-model and Waterfall approaches. Highlight some benefits (like their structure and clear definitions of requirements and budget), and drawbacks (like ongoing support being difficult to define with these structures).
- Introduce more cyclical/iterative approaches, like Spiral and Agile methodologies. Emphasize the moden update cycles that associates may already be familiar with, and relate them to the iterative approaches.
- Emphasize Agile methodologies, and introduce the "[Agile Manifesto](https://agilemanifesto.org/principles.html)". *NOTE: Agile is NOT an SDLC process, it is a family of processes that have similar supporting ideologies.*
- Focus the discussion on [Scrum Processes](https://aws.amazon.com/what-is/scrum/#:~:text=Scrum%20is%20a%20management%20framework,experience%2C%20and%20adapt%20to%20change) including:
    - Roles for members of the Scrum team (Product Owner, Scrum Leader (depreciate the archaic use of "Master"), Stakeholder, etc.)
    - Scrum ceremonies (Sprint planning, Stand-ups, Review, Retrospective, etc.)
    - Artifacts (Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, Burndown Chart, etc.)
    - Common tools (project/Kanban boards, velocity/story pointing, etc.)
- Relate the development process to an example of experience associates may already have, like planning a family gathering, moving, remodeling, maintaining a car, or making a big purchase. The decision making process and sprint ceremonies can generally be identified or equated in comparison to personal experience.
*NOTE: When introducing SDLC and Agile/Scrum process is an opportune time to introduce DevOps as a way of thinking, not just a set of tools. Consider ways to include the ideas of a DevOps methodology (removing the barriers between Development and Operations to make development a continuous process throughout the life of a product) in discussions about the Software Development Life Cycle.*

### Learning Objectives
- Emphasize the similarities between ALL development processes.
    - Documentation: all processes produce artifacts of some kind, even "Big Bang" methologies still produce SOMETHING to show at the end of it. Those artifacts allow developers to show progress on a task.
    - Accountability: for both team and individual accountability, a development process helps track that everyone on a team, and the team in general, is accomplishing the goals they set.
    - Commonality: development frameworks provide developers an understanding of the overall process and the teams progress, and allow teams to integrate new members quickly.

# What is a Full Stack Developer?

- Full stack technology refers to the entire depth of a computer system application, and full stack developers straddle two separate development domains: the front end and the back end.
- The front end includes everything that a client or site viewer can see and interact with.
- By contrast, the back  end refers to all the servers, databases, and other internal architecture that drives the application.
- The end-user typically does not interact with this realm directly.
- Those knowledgeable in both front-end and back-end development are called full-stack developers, meaning they are well-versed in both disciplines.
- Today, modern businesses often rely on entire teams of developers to operate network equipment, work with virtual machines, and manage enormous databases.
- It takes time to develop a comprehensive, nuts-and-bolts understanding of all these emerging technologies.
- The developers who do so are, for that reason, versatile enough to shift fluidly between front and back-end development and take on any task that their team might need them to tackle.

# Front End Work
- Front-end developers work to optimize the end-user visible parts of an application.
- This is typically some form of web development: creating the web pages that users will interact with and mapping user interactions to work with the application.

# Back End Work
- Back-end developers, in contrast, refine the software code that communicates with servers, databases, or other proprietary software that conveys information to front-end interfaces.

</details>

<details><summary>Real World Application</summary>
<br>

- The easiest way to put the full stack into perspective is to imagine a restaurant.

    - The front end encompasses the well-decorated, comfortable seating areas where visitors enjoy their food.
    - The kitchen and pantry make up the “back end” and are typically hidden away from the customer’s view.
    - Chefs (developers) gather permanently stored materials from the pantry (the database) and perform operations on them in the kitchen (the server), and then serve up fully prepared meals (information) to the user.


- The term “full stack developer” originated during the early days of the web, when websites were small and uncomplicated enough to allow a single person to tackle every aspect of site-building.

    - But in the decades since those initial days, the web has grown ever more complex.
    - The rise of machine learning, predictive computing, and responsive design has made it challenging — but not impossible! — for a single developer to handle every aspect of building and designing a site or application.


- According to a 2021 Stack Overflow survey of developers worldwide, roughly 49.47% identify as full stack.

</details>
<details><summary>Implementation</summary>
<br>

- The work of a full-stack developer will differ depending on the systems the front and back ends use, but the general type of work will be the same. When working on the back end, developers must ensure that data received from the front end is transformed and validated correctly. Any interactions with databases must be sanitized, and result sets returned from the database must also go through a transformation process to ensure the data fits what the front  end is expecting.

- In the front end developers need to ensure that users have an intuitive and secure interface to work with, typically through the use of web pages. These should be hosted on a secure server that has permission to make requests to the back end. When the user makes requests to the back end, the data returned must be transformed into meaningful information displayed on the web page, if applicable.

- Again how this plays out will vary depending on the software you work with and the services you are building. Fundamentally, your front end will facilitate user interactions and your back end will process those actions.

</details>
